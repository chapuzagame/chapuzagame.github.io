Política de privacidad

Esta política explica qué datos se tratan cuando usas la app How to Pour Beer para Android (y la versión web del juego). Responsable: el desarrollador de la app, que puedes contactar en chapuzagame@gmail.com.

Lo que guarda el juego

Tu progreso (puntos, logros, tu bar, el nombre que pongas en el carnet, idioma y ajustes) se guarda solo en tu dispositivo. No hay cuentas ni registro, y el desarrollador no recibe esos datos. Si desinstalas la app o borras sus datos, se pierden (salvo la copia de seguridad de Android, si la tienes activada en tu cuenta de Google).

Anuncios (Google AdMob)

La app muestra anuncios de Google AdMob. Para mostrarlos, medirlos y evitar fraudes, el SDK de Google puede recoger datos de tu dispositivo: el identificador de publicidad, la dirección IP (y con ella una ubicación aproximada), información del dispositivo y de la app, y cómo interactúas con los anuncios. Google trata esos datos según su política de privacidad y explica cómo los usa en Cómo utiliza Google la información de los sitios o aplicaciones que utilizan sus servicios.

Si estás en el Espacio Económico Europeo, el Reino Unido o Suiza, al abrir la app se te pide tu consentimiento (con el formulario de Google). Si no lo das, se pueden mostrar anuncios no personalizados o ninguno. Puedes cambiar tu decisión cuando quieras en Tu ficha → Privacidad de los anuncios. También puedes restablecer o desactivar el ID de publicidad en los ajustes de Android.

Compras dentro de la app (Google Play)

Las compras las procesa Google Play. El desarrollador no recibe tus datos de pago: la app solo recibe la confirmación de qué has comprado para darte lo comprado y poder restaurarlo. Más información en la política de privacidad de Google.

Edad

El juego trata de servir cerveza y está pensado para mayores de 18 años. No está dirigido a menores.

Tus derechos

Según el RGPD puedes pedir acceso, rectificación, supresión, oposición, limitación o portabilidad de tus datos escribiendo a chapuzagame@gmail.com, y reclamar ante la Agencia Española de Protección de Datos (aepd.es). Como el desarrollador no guarda datos tuyos en ningún servidor, para los datos de anuncios y pagos tendrás que dirigirte a Google.

Privacy policy

This policy explains what data is processed when you use the How to Pour Beer app for Android (and the web version of the game). Controller: the app's developer, who you can contact at chapuzagame@gmail.com.

What the game stores

Your progress (score, achievements, your bar, the name on your card, language and settings) is stored only on your device. There are no accounts or sign-up, and the developer does not receive this data. If you uninstall the app or clear its data, it is lost (except for Android's backup, if enabled on your Google account).

Ads (Google AdMob)

The app shows ads from Google AdMob. To show and measure them and to prevent fraud, Google's SDK may collect data from your device: the advertising ID, IP address (and an approximate location derived from it), device and app information, and how you interact with ads. Google processes this data under its privacy policy and explains its use in How Google uses information from sites or apps that use its services.

If you are in the European Economic Area, the UK or Switzerland, the app asks for your consent when it opens (with Google's form). If you don't consent, non-personalised ads or no ads may be shown. You can change your choice at any time in Your card → Ad privacy settings. You can also reset or turn off the advertising ID in Android's settings.

In-app purchases (Google Play)

Purchases are processed by Google Play. The developer does not receive your payment details: the app only gets confirmation of what you bought, so it can unlock and restore it. See Google's privacy policy.

Age

The game is about pouring beer and is intended for people aged 18 and over. It is not directed at children.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you can request access, rectification, erasure, objection, restriction or portability by writing to chapuzagame@gmail.com, and complain to your data protection authority. Since the developer keeps no data about you on any server, for ad and payment data you will need to contact Google.

The website (chapuzagame.github.io)

The website and the browser version of the game count visits with Umami, without cookies: only anonymous, aggregate statistics (pages viewed, country, device type and browser, which site or social network you came from —and, if the link is ours, from which post— and, in the demo, which modes are played, how the beers in each round turn out, on which screen it's left and how many days have passed since the first time, whether the end is reached, how far players get in My bar and for how long, whether they come back another day and complete the daily challenges, whether a challenge is shared or opened, whether the link to the full game is clicked and whether the button to test the app before release is clicked). When you share a challenge, you send it yourself (your score and the link) with the app you choose. The link also carries a random code from your device and the time you sent it, only so that an old challenge doesn't count against a newer one; it doesn't say who you are, and the challenges you send and open are noted only on your device. If you join the testers group (Google Groups), as the group's owners we see your email address; we only use it for the Google Play test and you can leave the group at any time. If you email us to test the app before release, we only use your email to invite you to the Google Play test and delete it when the test ends. They are not used for advertising, not combined with other data and cannot identify you. The site is hosted on GitHub Pages, which, like any server, receives your IP address when it loads.

App statistics

The Android app also counts, with Umami and without cookies or identifiers (neither the advertising ID nor any other), anonymous aggregate statistics to learn what gets played and what breaks: that the app is opened, which modes are played, the first steps the first time (starting, finishing the first beer and the first game), how the beers in each round turn out and which level is reached, on which screen the app is left and how many days have passed since the first time, where the install came from (what Google Play says: the social network or our website, or a search in the store), how far players get in My bar, when they sell up and for how long they play, whether they come back another day and complete the daily challenges, whether the store is opened, which product someone tries to buy and whether the purchase goes through, whether a challenge is shared or opened, whether there is an update and whether it's tapped, whether beta reminders are turned on and opened, whether Google Play Games is opened, and the game's error messages if something fails, along with the app version, language and screen size. Umami receives your IP address when they are sent, uses it to work out the country and doesn't store it. They are not used for advertising, not combined with other data and cannot identify you. You can turn them off at any time in the app: Your card → Anonymous stats. If you email us with the feedback button, the email includes the game version, your phone model and Android version so we can reproduce the problem; you see it before sending it.

Google Play Games

If you have a Google Play Games profile on your phone, the app records the achievements you earn there, so you can also see them in Play Games. That's handled by Google under its privacy policy; we don't receive or store anything from your profile.

Beta reminders

While the app is in closed testing, it asks whether you'd like an occasional reminder to play. If you say yes (and allow notifications), the reminders are scheduled on your own phone: no server sends them or knows who gets them. You can turn them off in Your card → Beta reminders or in your phone's settings. To know whether the beta is still on and how many days are left, the app reads a small file from our website when it opens (chapuzagame.github.io/beta/estado.json); that request carries no data about you, although GitHub Pages, like any server, receives your IP address.
